## Voiceover

Meet Dave. He runs a busy trade office. His tea has never been drunk hot.

Every morning brings enquiries, ringing phones, and three kinds of message: urgent, very urgent, and "just a quick one."

Where's the van? What's happening on site? Was that extra work recorded? And the part everyone thought someone else ordered? Nobody ordered it.

Then it's Friday. Time for the timesheet treasure hunt. A text. Two spreadsheets. And what appears to be a sandwich receipt.

Dave needs a better system. Meet Jobs Tracker. Your office, engineers and vans, connected in one place.

Enquiries become quotes, then jobs, without re-keying everything. Conversations stay with the job, so the office and the team can find the same answers.

Photos and updates come straight from site. See what's happening, capture variations, and record materials and purchase order numbers while the details are fresh. That extra work stays visible.

Vans, engineers and jobs appear on one live map, using the trackers you already have. Dave can retire the detective routine.

GPS tap-in and tap-out builds timesheets automatically. Review, approve, then export to Sage or Xero. Friday gets a lot less interesting. Dave is delighted.

Less chasing. Less retyping. More time for the business, and a life after work. Dave even gets a hot cup of tea.

Jobs Tracker. From dispatch to done. Book your demo at jobs tracker dot work.

## Performance, music and motion

- Narration: conversational delivery with understated comic timing. The approved go-ahead was followed with Higgsfield's Arthur preset. The source take is approximately 87 seconds, with scene pauses added to reach the two-minute film length.
- Music: playful, lightly percussive opening, building with the office chaos. A brief rhythmic stop gives the timesheet gag space. At the product reveal, open into a warm, optimistic instrumental theme. Keep music below the voice; fade gently under the final CTA.
- Sound design, if available: restrained notification pings, paper shuffles, clock tick, tidy confirmation sounds and a small mug clink. Avoid repetitive alarms or a laugh track. Visual gags must also work without effects.
- Motion: deliberate character reactions and accumulating objects in the problem half; connected flows, organised records and clean reveals in the solution half. Return to the same desk and manager for the final payoff.
- Timing: 120 seconds is the target, including pauses and the end-card hold. Fit the final selected voice take to scene windows; do not squeeze narration into a fixed window by making it sound rushed.
