# Pitch deck content slides 1 to 4

Use this outline to build a 12 slide investor presentation after the evidence fields are completed. Keep one main claim on each slide and place its evidence in the speaker notes or source footer.

## Slide 1 Company and proposed round
Supercars.Tours. Independent comparisons and supplier discovery for supercar owners. UK operations. Intended raise €700k to €1m, with final terms to be agreed. Show the website and founder contact once confirmed. Avoid first, only, leading or largest claims.

Suggested visual: a genuine current product screenshot with a date. Label any mockup clearly.

## Slide 2 Owner problem
Present one documented owner decision and the information needed to compare alternatives. Separate the tour comparison problem from supplier discovery. Add findings from owner interviews with dates, participant count and limitations. Until interviews exist, label the problem as the founding hypothesis.

Suggested visual: the actual steps taken in one researched owner journey.

## Slide 3 Product demonstration
Show an operator comparison and a supplier profile. Identify what works today, what is in development and what is planned. Explain driving and luxury dimensions and how missing information appears. Do not use a polished concept image as proof that a feature is live.

Suggested visual: two genuine screens annotated with source dates and feature status.

## Slide 4 Paying customer and initial market
Show the initial UK supplier categories and the owner intent each serves. Distinguish the wider enthusiast audience from users with a service or tour decision. Explain which category is being tested first and why the team can recruit and serve it.

Suggested visual: a small matrix of pilot categories, target owner needs and current supplier evidence. Add a bottom up estimate only after deduplicating eligible suppliers and validating realistic spend.

## Evidence required before design is final
Product feature inventory, screenshots, founder approved company description, owner interviews, supplier mapping and publication permission for any named business. The team should be able to trace every numeric claim to the evidence register.

# Pitch deck content slides 5 to 8

## Slide 5 Commercial model
Show who pays, what they receive and when payment occurs. Present annual listings first, with sponsorship, accepted leads and premium visibility as distinct services. Use the original proposed price ranges and identify which prices, if any, have been accepted by customers.

Suggested visual: four revenue streams with a short definition of each deliverable. Explain that visibility purchases do not change editorial scores.

## Slide 6 Traction and validation
Show dated paying supplier counts, cash received, relevant owner activity, accepted leads and renewals. Define active users and distinguish invoices from receipts. When a metric is not yet available, show the planned test rather than a fabricated result.

Suggested visual: a dated pilot funnel from actual users to enquiries and accepted leads. Report rejected leads and paid acquisition costs alongside successes.

## Slide 7 Competition and differentiation
Compare named, reviewed alternatives across owner use case, comparable information, geographic coverage, commercial model and treatment of advertising. Distinguish general search, clubs and communities, specialist media, directories and operator websites.

Suggested visual: a concise evidence based comparison. The current proposed differentiation is transparent comparisons and a focused owner supplier audience. It is not yet a demonstrated barrier to entry.

## Slide 8 Acquisition and economics
Explain how the first UK users and paying suppliers are acquired. Use actual channel spend, sales effort, accepted lead value and early retention when available. State when customer acquisition cost includes staff time and when it excludes it.

Suggested visual: one measured acquisition experiment. Do not present lifetime value based on an assumed renewal rate as an observed result. The model's retention, session conversion and paid lead assumptions remain illustrations.

## Questions the presentation must answer
Why would an owner return? What does a supplier gain beyond a general directory? How much editorial work is required per profile? Can the acquisition process work outside the founder's relationships? What happens to revenue when a sponsor leaves? Attach evidence or a specific test to each unresolved question.

# Pitch deck content slides 9 to 12

## Slide 9 Team and delivery capacity
Present the founder, Charlie as COO and Lorenzo Granay using agreed titles and approved biographies. Show relevant operating, product, editorial and sales experience with dates or verifiable examples. Identify the technical and editorial roles still to fill.

Do not list the Ireland or Spain prospects as team members until they agree and their roles are established. Lorenzo's Italy location does not establish an Italian business launch.

## Slide 10 Milestones and deployment
Show a sequence from accurate directory and comparison data to paid supplier pilots, repeatable acquisition and renewals. Assign each milestone an owner, budget, period and acceptance evidence. Make the plan achievable at the minimum accepted raise.

Suggested visual: the funded roadmap. App development, route planning and further countries should appear only where justified and costed.

## Slide 11 Financial plan and funding need
Show three annual forecast columns with recognised revenue, operating costs and closing cash, sourced from the workbook. State that the forecast is illustrative until management validates it. Explain annual subscription cash collection separately from revenue recognition.

Use the gross to net proceeds table and identify the proposed cash reserve. Include the principal downside assumptions. No forecast should imply guaranteed profit or that an investor can readily sell shares.

## Slide 12 Proposed offer and material risks
State the final approved gross target, minimum close, valuation, share class, investor rights and use of any overfunding once agreed. Currently the only founder specified funding term is the intended €700k to €1m range.

Include material risks covering demand, execution, supplier concentration, editorial trust, data handling, dilution, loss of capital and illiquidity. Use the selected platform's approved risk wording and campaign link. Omit tax relief claims until eligibility and permitted wording are confirmed.

## Presenter notes
Keep answers aligned with the campaign page, financial model and offer documents. If evidence changes, update every version. Do not rely on speaking notes to correct a misleading headline. Before the deck is circulated as an investment promotion, obtain the route specific review required by the platform and advisers.
