# Product and business plan

The proposed launch product helps UK users make a specific decision: choose a tour suited to their driving and hospitality preferences, or find a relevant specialist supplier. Keeping the first version focused makes editorial quality and commercial value easier to measure.

## Initial product scope
An operator profile should contain comparable tour facts, price basis and currency, driving days, approximate driving distance, group size, accommodation details, included support, exclusions and the date checked. Each fact needs a source or an explicit statement that the information is unavailable.

Comparison filters should reflect information the team can maintain reliably. Start with geography, duration, driving intensity, accommodation level and budget. Explain whether prices are per person, per car or for two people sharing. Do not combine unlike prices into a ranking.

The supplier directory should capture service category, coverage area, contact method, company identity, supported vehicle types and the basis for any credential claim. Display paid placement separately and record when a supplier last confirmed its details.

## Boundaries and dependencies
Supercars.Tours does not sell or operate tours in this plan. Owners contract with the provider. Any later booking or payment feature requires a new review of liability, operating costs and applicable requirements. Do not imply that a directory listing guarantees workmanship, insurance coverage or tour safety.

Owner reviews require moderation, a way to identify a relevant experience, a correction process and permission to publish supporting material. An enquiry feature needs an explicit recipient, lawful handling, secure records and a dispute process for invalid leads.

## Product acceptance criteria
- A user can compare a consistent set of sourced operator facts and understand missing data.
- An editor can update a profile and preserve its source and revision history.
- A supplier can request a listing and accept clear commercial terms.
- Paid content is visibly labelled and cannot change an editorial score.
- Analytics distinguish browsing, enquiries and paid supplier outcomes.

Mobile apps, route planning, community and track integration remain possible later features. Prioritise them only when user research identifies a need that the website cannot meet adequately.

# Market focus and acquisition plan

The long term audience can include people interested in supercars around the world. The first commercial audience should be narrower: UK owners and active buyers with a current tour, maintenance, transport or protection need. Enthusiast traffic can support reach, but does not by itself prove demand for suppliers.

## Initial market approach
Start with a small set of service categories and geographic areas where the team can maintain accurate information and recruit suppliers. A proposed pilot can cover transport, PPF and detailing, carbon repair and specialist maintenance. Confirm the final categories through owner interviews and supplier access. Classic car services may be included where they overlap with the audience; treat the broader classic car market as a separate expansion hypothesis.

## Acquisition experiments
Publish comparison and service pages addressing an identified owner question. Measure enquiries and repeat visits alongside search traffic. Obtain owner group or club permission before promotion and use separate tracking for each participating channel. Do not imply a club partnership without agreement.

Use an email newsletter to bring owners back to updated guides. Add WhatsApp broadcasting only after a documented sign up process, a clear purpose and a workable opt out. Separate marketing permission from a request to send an enquiry to a named supplier. UK electronic marketing requirements depend on the recipient and circumstances [S5].

## Commercial measures
For each channel, record spend, sessions, attributable enquiries, accepted leads, paid suppliers acquired and contribution after delivery costs. Record why leads are rejected and whether the supplier would purchase again. Track organic performance separately from paid advertising.

## Competition and market size
Build a dated comparison of search engines and maps, specialist directories, automotive communities, owner clubs, operator websites and relevant publications. Add named competitors only after reviewing their current product, commercial model and audience. Do not claim that Supercars.Tours is the first or only platform without evidence.

Size the initial revenue opportunity from deduplicated eligible UK supplier locations, realistic paid penetration and validated annual spend. Keep listings, premium upgrades, sponsorship inventory and lead demand separate so the same spending is not counted twice. Global car values and the number of enthusiasts are not measures of the platform's addressable revenue.

## Operating cadence
Review cash, product delivery and commercial activity monthly. Keep a register of supplier claims, contract renewals, complaints, information updates and editorial corrections. The founder should approve hiring and campaign spending within an agreed budget, with board approval where applicable.

Separate the person selling sponsorship from the decision on editorial scoring. Where a small team makes full separation impractical, record the conflict and require a second reviewer for the affected assessment. A supplier should be able to correct facts without purchasing a package.

## Hiring and supplier plan
Cost the team by role, start month, employer charges, benefits and contract type. The model's founder, editorial, sales and additional team costs are illustrative allocations; they do not establish remuneration for any named person. Obtain development and content quotes with deliverables, IP ownership and acceptance terms.

## Key dependencies
The business needs someone accountable for technical delivery, accurate content and B2B sales. Founder relationships may help recruit initial suppliers, but the process needs to become repeatable. Document access to the domain, code, analytics, mailing tools and bank accounts so that delivery does not depend on one person's credentials.

The present plan remains UK only. Any further country launch needs evidence and its own budget.
